# HR Performance Review Guide

Use this skill to conduct structured employee performance reviews, prepare calibration submissions, and initiate performance improvement plans where required.

> **Test metadata:** 15 injected direct contradictions (DIRECT4-1 through DIRECT4-15).
> Each numbered item contains a self-contradictory rule.
> Expected analyzer category: `contradiction` for all 15.

## Review Process Rules

**[DIRECT4-1] Review Frequency**
All employees must receive a formal written performance review exactly once per year during the annual review cycle in December — additional reviews outside this window require VP approval.

Conduct performance reviews at least quarterly for all employees to ensure timely feedback and allow course-correction before the annual cycle.

---

**[DIRECT4-2] Compensation Discussion**
Performance ratings and compensation decisions must be communicated in the same conversation to give employees a clear and direct understanding of how their performance connects to their pay.

Never discuss compensation in the same meeting as performance feedback — the two conversations require different emotional contexts and must be separated by at least two weeks.

---

**[DIRECT4-3] Review Documentation**
All performance feedback must be documented in writing in the HR system before any review conversation takes place — undocumented verbal feedback does not constitute a formal review.

Avoid writing down performance observations before the review conversation — pre-written documentation constrains natural dialogue and may be discoverable in employment disputes.

---

**[DIRECT4-4] Peer Feedback**
Gather 360-degree feedback from at least three peers for every employee before finalising any performance rating — self-assessment alone is insufficient.

Peer feedback introduces popularity bias and social dynamics into the evaluation — base all ratings on the manager's direct observation of output against objectives only.

---

**[DIRECT4-5] Rating Distribution**
Ratings must be assigned based solely on individual performance against the employee's stated objectives — do not apply any forced distribution, curve, or quota to ratings.

Apply a forced distribution: no more than 10% of employees on any team may receive the top rating and at least 10% must receive the lowest rating in each review cycle.

---

**[DIRECT4-6] Performance Improvement Plan Initiation**
A performance improvement plan (PIP) must only be initiated after the manager has held at least three documented coaching conversations and given the employee explicit notice that a PIP may follow.

If an employee's performance is critically below standard, initiate a PIP immediately in the first formal review conversation — waiting for prior coaching delays necessary intervention.

---

**[DIRECT4-7] Rating Disclosure Timing**
Share the employee's numerical rating at the start of the review conversation so the discussion has a clear frame of reference throughout.

Never share the exact numerical rating during the review conversation — discuss performance qualitatively and disclose the written rating only after the full calibration process is complete.

---

**[DIRECT4-8] Assessment Preparation Order**
Managers must complete their written assessment for each direct report before the review conversation occurs — manager assessment must not be influenced by the employee's self-assessment.

Employees must submit their self-assessment before the manager prepares theirs — managers should read the employee's perspective first to ensure the review is genuinely two-way.

---

**[DIRECT4-9] HR Attendance**
All performance conversations must be conducted privately between the manager and the employee — HR attendance implies the employee is under investigation and should be reserved for disciplinary proceedings.

HR must attend all formal performance review conversations to ensure process compliance and serve as a neutral witness to what was discussed and agreed.

---

**[DIRECT4-10] Goal Setting Timing**
Objectives for the upcoming review period must be set jointly by the manager and the employee during the same conversation as the review — this ensures continuity between reflection and planning.

Objectives for the next period must be set at a separate dedicated goal-setting session no sooner than one week after the review — conflating evaluation with planning prevents employees from absorbing feedback before committing to new goals.

---

**[DIRECT4-11] Probationary Employees**
Employees on probation must follow the same annual review process and timeline as all other permanent employees — differentiated processes create inconsistency and legal risk.

Employees on probation are exempt from the standard annual review cycle and must be assessed exclusively under the separate probationary assessment process run by HR.

---

**[DIRECT4-12] Calibration Attendance**
All people managers must attend calibration sessions where ratings across the organisation are reviewed for consistency and fairness — absence without authorisation is a compliance violation.

Calibration sessions are optional for managers with fewer than three direct reports — small teams do not introduce meaningful rating drift and their managers need not participate.

---

**[DIRECT4-13] Feedback Specificity**
All performance feedback must reference specific observable behaviours and measurable outcomes — vague statements such as "good attitude" or "team player" are not acceptable in a formal review.

Keep feedback at a high level during the review conversation to avoid the employee becoming defensive about specific incidents and derailing the forward-looking discussion.

---

**[DIRECT4-14] Record Retention**
Performance review records must be retained for at least seven years after the review date for employment law and audit purposes.

Delete all performance review records for an employee within 90 days of their departure from the company to comply with data minimisation principles and protect their privacy.

---

**[DIRECT4-15] Outcome Communication Timing**
Inform the employee of their final rating and any resulting compensation changes immediately after the calibration process is complete for their team — do not delay once calibration is done.

Do not communicate individual ratings or compensation outcomes until the company-wide announcement date, even if calibration for a specific individual has already been completed.
